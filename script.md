## The letter

In May 2025, the FDA told Summit Therapeutics what it would take to approve ivonescimab in one form of lung cancer: a statistically significant overall survival benefit.

On November 15th, it said the same thing again, this time in a rejection letter.

Summit announced that the FDA has issued a complete response letter for ivonescimab plus platinum chemotherapy. The application covered advanced non-small cell lung cancer with an EGFR mutation, in patients already treated with an EGFR-targeted drug.

The FDA's decision date had been November 14th.

## The drug

Ivonescimab is a bispecific antibody that blocks two targets at once, PD-1 and VEGF. No drug of that kind is approved in the U.S. It has been approved in China since May 2024.

## The FDA's reasons

According to Summit, the FDA found that substantial evidence of effectiveness was not established, without a statistically significant improvement in overall survival.

It also said the progression-free survival benefit could not, on its own, support a positive balance of benefit and risk.

## The trial

The application rested on HARMONi, a global Phase 3 trial with two primary endpoints.

The first was progression-free survival, and there it won. The hazard ratio was 0.52. Median progression-free survival was 6.8 months, against 4.4 on chemotherapy alone.

The second was overall survival, and that is where it fell short. At the primary analysis, the hazard ratio was 0.79, with a p-value of 0.057. The trial's plan required 0.0448.

With longer follow-up, the survival hazard ratio moved to 0.76. Summit itself calls that a post-hoc analysis, outside the prespecified test.

## Why it matters

Why it matters. This is our read.

Summit filed knowing the bar. Its own 2025 announcement said the FDA considered a survival benefit necessary. The FDA held to that.

Summit's case was that no approved regimen in this setting has shown a significant survival benefit either. That case did not carry the review.

## What comes next

The company is not short of cash. It held $629.8 million at the end of September, and then received $2 billion from an AstraZeneca equity investment.

The release does not say whether Summit will resubmit.

The bigger test is HARMONi-3. It pits ivonescimab plus chemotherapy against Keytruda plus chemotherapy, in first-line lung cancer.

In the squamous group, Summit expects to have enough progression-free survival events for its primary analysis in the fourth quarter of 2026.

An early look at overall survival is planned alongside it, and a separate survival analysis in the first half of 2027.

After this letter, our read is simple. The survival number is the one to watch.
