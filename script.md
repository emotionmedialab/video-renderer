## The headline

[stat: $5.9B | up to, in milestones]
Eli Lilly's new cancer deal with Stipple Bio is worth up to $5.9 billion.

[emphasize: "neither company published"]
That is the headline. The number that matters is the one neither company published.

On November 16, Stipple announced a research collaboration and license agreement with Lilly, covering multiple targets and multiple types of drugs.

## The terms

Here is the money, in order. Stipple gets an upfront payment, and Lilly makes an equity investment in the company.

[emphasize: "has not been disclosed"]
How big either one is has not been disclosed.

Everything after that is conditional. The $5.9 billion is the ceiling on research, development and commercial milestones, paid only as programs reach them. On top of that, tiered royalties if a product is ever sold.

In return, Lilly gets exclusive worldwide rights to the products that come out of the collaboration.

## The platform

[emphasize: "a way to find targets"]
So Lilly is not buying a drug. It is buying a way to find targets.

Stipple's Pointillist platform looks for binders to tumor-specific epitopes on the cell surface. An epitope is the exact spot on a protein where an antibody attaches. Stipple says these binders are designed to hit tumor cells selectively.

The aim is to avoid on-target, off-tumor toxicity: a drug that finds its target in healthy tissue too.

Lilly will pair those binders with its own oncology technologies, across more than one modality.

## The company

[stat: $100M | Series A, April]
Stipple is a private biotech in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It came out of stealth in April with a $100 million Series A, co-led by RA Capital, a16z Bio+Health, and Nextech Invest.

[emphasize: "early 2027"]
Its lead drug, STP-100, is an antibody-drug conjugate. Stipple says the Lilly deal lets it keep advancing its own pipeline, with a Phase 1 trial of STP-100 planned for early 2027, in solid tumors including pancreatic cancer.

The announcement names no targets for the Lilly work, gives no count, and describes none of it as clinical.

## Our read

Why it matters. This is our read.

[emphasize: "not what Lilly has paid"]
When the ceiling is public and the upfront is private, the headline tells you how big success could be, not what Lilly has paid.

Lilly is paying to come in at target discovery, before any program in this deal has been described as reaching patients. Most of the $5.9 billion only exists if the science works, program by program.

And the equity matters. Lilly is not only a partner. It is also an investor in the company whose platform it is betting on.

## What to watch

So the signal comes in order. First, whether either company ever discloses the upfront. That is the real price of entry.

Then, STP-100's Phase 1 start in early 2027, when Stipple's lead drug is due to be tested in people for the first time.

[emphasize: "a ceiling, not a payment"]
And then, the first named program under the Lilly deal. Until that happens, $5.9 billion is a ceiling, not a payment.
