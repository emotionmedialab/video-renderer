## The readout

[stat: 68% | lower risk of recurrence]
CG Oncology says its bladder cancer drug cut the risk of the cancer coming back by 68% in a Phase 3 trial.

On November 16, the company reported topline results from PIVOT-006. The trial met its primary endpoint.

[emphasize: "December 2"]
The details are not public yet. CG Oncology will present them on December 2, as a late-breaking talk at the Society of Urologic Oncology annual meeting. So here is what is known now, and what to look for then.

## The drug

The drug is cretostimogene grenadenorepvec, an engineered virus delivered directly into the bladder.

It is built to replicate in tumor cells where a growth-control pathway called RB is disrupted, and it carries a gene for GM-CSF, an immune signal. It is not approved anywhere.

## The trial

PIVOT-006 enrolled patients with intermediate-risk non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer, plus small high-grade TA tumors: cancer in the bladder lining that has not reached the muscle wall.

[stat: 50,000 | patients, company estimate]
These tumors are removed in surgery, but they can come back. CG Oncology puts this group at about 50,000 patients, and says no FDA-approved adjuvant treatment exists for them.

[stat: 367 | patients, ~90 sites]
The trial enrolled 367 patients at about 90 sites in the U.S. and Canada.

After surgery, they were randomized to cretostimogene or to surveillance alone, in an open-label design.

## The numbers

[stat: 0.32 | hazard ratio, recurrence-free survival]
The hazard ratio for recurrence-free survival was 0.32, which is where the 68% comes from. Median follow-up was 17 months.

On safety, the company reported no grade 3 or higher treatment-related side effects, and no patients stopping treatment because of one.

## What's missing

[emphasize: "not yet presented or peer-reviewed"]
These are company-reported topline results, not yet presented or peer-reviewed.

The release gives no recurrence rates for each arm, no event counts, and no separate high-grade or low-grade figures. The company says the benefit held in both, without the numbers.

## Our read

Why it matters. This is our read.

[emphasize: "surveillance, not another drug"]
The comparison was surveillance, not another drug. Against watching and waiting, a hazard ratio of 0.32 would be a large effect if the full data hold up. December 2nd should show how large, in which patients, and for how long.

It also reshapes the filing plan. CG Oncology's first FDA application, in patients whose cancer no longer responds to the standard treatment BCG, was due this quarter. After FDA feedback it now covers more patients, with completion expected by the end of March 2027.

A second application, built on PIVOT-006, is planned for the second quarter of 2027.

## What to watch

Three markers, in order. First, December 2nd: the recurrence-free rates in each arm, the event count, and the high-grade and low-grade results on their own.

Then, the two filings, on the dates the company has set.

[emphasize: "a filing is not an approval"]
And a filing is not an approval. That decision sits with the FDA.
